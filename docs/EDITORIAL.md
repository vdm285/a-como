# How the stage speaks

Written 2026-10-21, revised after each of the four self-audits that night and the next morning. This is the rule the app follows for every number and word on a screen. When a rule here and the code disagree, one of them is a bug.

We do not promise that a price is right today. We promise that every price names who recorded it and when, and that every comparison names its reference.

## Prices and time

- **Prices are PROFECO's**, as published in "Quién es Quién en los Precios": label price per product per store per visit. PROFECO surveys weekly and publishes monthly, five to nine weeks behind; the app says so in Settings and never calls old data "today".
- **Recent** means the last 28 days of data (`meta.recent`, for example 4–31 Aug 2026). The default view uses each store's latest price inside that window, with its date. A store whose latest price is older is a quiet ring, and its tooltip says when PROFECO last saw the product there. We never carry an old price into "recent", never average across weeks, never interpolate a week.
- **A chosen week** uses only that week's observations. The timeline draws every week of the data (88 for a product) and lets the reader pick the last 26; the older weeks are shaded. A store PROFECO did not visit that week is a quiet ring that says "sin dato esa semana".
- **Light weeks** (`meta.partialWeeks`: under half the median week's observations, such as the last Monday of the file or the Christmas weeks) are marked on the timeline and labelled "semana con pocos datos". A store's recent index skips them: 53 stores on one Monday are too thin a baseline.
- **Dates carry the year** whenever it is not the year of the latest data.
- **A link names the week on screen.** A `?w=` outside the data becomes the week shown (the nearest earlier one, else the first); a malformed one is dropped, so "Copiar liga" never shares a week the reader did not see.

## Comparisons

- **One reference per stage**: the city median of the recent window, stated on the legend ("mediana reciente de la ciudad: $19.00"). Scrubbing the timeline colours every week against that same reference, so movement shows. Because a dear week turns every dot the same red, the week's city value is printed large over the map while the week is on stage.
- **One card grammar** for a product, a generic product and the basket: the cheapest store first with its price and date, the reference named once under it (in a chosen week, that week's city median and the recent one), the strip of every store, then the ranked list.
- **The median, never the mean.** A chip ("31 % abajo") needs a reference from at least three stores. With fewer, there is no chip, the dots are neutral grey and the legend says the comparison is not possible.
- **The store index** is the median, over the products PROFECO saw in the store that week, of the store's price divided by the city median of the same product that week. A product counts only when at least three stores reported it that week (with one or two, the "median" is the store's own price), and a store needs at least 30 such products, so tortillerías and bakeries have none. The stage asks it as a question, "¿Qué tan cara es cada tienda?", and answers in words, "3 % más cara". Because a chain whose prices are often the city median (every Walmart) lands on exactly 1.000, the store card also gives the split of its recent prices against the city, over the same products as its wins and losses (medicines only in a pharmacy, and then the sentence says so): "306 más baratos, 296 iguales y 396 más caros, de 998 productos, sin contar medicinas"; the card explains it under "Cómo se calcula" and names the week of the index it shows. When a later week was left out because it was light, the card says so ("la semana del 31 ago no cuenta: la ciudad tuvo menos de la mitad de los datos de costumbre").
- **A generic product** compares only members sold in at least 3 stores and within a third to three times the group's median size (a 200 ml chocolate box is not the city's dearest litre of milk, a grade sold in one store is not the cheapest jitomate); the others stay in the list, marked "fuera de la comparación" with the reason. In a chosen week, "3 stores" means 3 stores with a price that week (a grade with 8 recent stores and 2 that week is not that week's cheapest), and each member's row gives both counts ("8 tiendas · 2 esa semana") beside that member's median price that week, under "mediana esa semana". It compares them in one unit decided for the whole group (the dimension most members share; litres or kilos when the median member size is 700 ml or 700 g or more, else 100 ml or 100 g; pieces for eggs and rolls). Members in another unit are listed apart with their own per-unit price and are never coloured against the group's unit; members whose presentation has no size are listed apart by label price, marked "sin tamaño en el nombre". The group's reference is "la mediana de lo más barato en cada tienda", said in those words.
- **One colour scale**: blue cheaper, grey the median, red dearer, never red/green. The cap (±5 %, ±10 %…) is chosen once per stage from the recent spread, printed on the legend as "o más", and kept while the reader scrubs, so a saturated dot reads "at least this much".
- **"Revísalo"**: a price 2.5 times or more, or 0.4 times or less, the median of its own comparison set (with n ≥ 3) is shown as published with a "revísalo" flag: it may be a capture error or a real price, and it is never a store's win or loss, never a map tag, never a "cheapest". The rule is symmetric in log terms (a price at a third of the median is as suspect as one at three times it) and is decided once per stage (`v.far` in `src/app/stage.js`), so the card, the store card, the tooltip and the tags agree. The comparison set is the recent prices by default and that week's prices in a chosen week: in the April 2026 jitomate spike a $49 kilo was cheap for its week, not an error. The flag's hint names which set.
- **The hero** of a product, a generic product and the basket is the cheapest price that is not flagged "revísalo". When several stores share it, it says "la más barata, empatada en 41 tiendas", dated with the span of the tied stores' dates, never one store chosen by file order. Under 3 stores there is no superlative, but the store is named: "la única tienda con precio: X", "X, una de solo 2 tiendas con precio", "X, una de solo 2 tiendas con todo"; two stores at the same price are a tie, "empatada en las 2 tiendas con precio". In such a thin set the prices set aside as "revísalo" are counted after the date ("1 precio más, marcado «revísalo», no cuenta"), so the line agrees with the counts beside it; in a long list the strip and the rows show the flags instead. When every price is flagged, the card says so and crowns nothing.
- **Ties on the map** are one tag: equal prices are one group, and a group of several stores is tagged once with "empatada en N tiendas"; clicking it opens the card on the tied rows and marks them. A "cheap" tag is below the reference and a "dear" one above it: the median itself is never tagged as cheap. In a chosen week the side is judged against that week's own median (in the April 2026 jitomate spike every price was above the recent one, and the map would have tagged no cheapest store); the colours keep the one recent reference.
- **The movement in words**: the product and generic-product cards state the highest and lowest weekly median with their weeks, from weeks with 3 stores that are not light weeks, when they differ by 10 % or more. The sentence names its rule ("semanas completas con 3 tiendas o más"), because its lowest week can sit above a recent median taken across stores. The timeline's line skips weeks under 3 stores and light weeks (both are faint dots), and its "pico" marks a peak only when it stands out from the typical week and is reached in at most two weeks: a value held, within 1 %, for three weeks or more is a plateau and gets no note (the card's sentence then says "13 semanas a menos de 1 % de él") (the card's sentence gives it with its count), and a value reached twice is dated to its latest week, as the card dates it. A curve with no week of 3 stores still draws its weeks as faint dots and its caption says "ninguna semana tuvo 3 tiendas".
- **A week's own figure needs 3 stores.** The large number over the map, the card's "mediana de la ciudad esa semana" and the slider's spoken value appear only when at least 3 stores give that week a price; with one or two, the card says how many stores had a price and nothing more.
- **Medicines** count in a store's wins, losses and split only when the store is a pharmacy; their spread swamps a supermarket's list (with them, La Comer Coyoacán read cheaper more often than dearer; without them, the opposite). The index itself still counts them: it is a median, and leaving them out moves it by 0 in the typical supermarket and by at most 1.3 points (measured on the recent prices of the 121 supermarkets with 50 or more medicines, October 2026).

- **The opening (Relieve)** follows the portfolio's rules (`docs/portfolio/SPEC.md`): every number from the data, computed as the stories say (matched stores, the recent window); motion between two real weeks is an eased tween that lands on the real heights, and a printed number never tweens; the label always names the real week. Its stories are computed at build time (`npm run opening`), never written by hand.

## Words and credits

- **Nothing invented.** No product photographs until a legal, matched source exists; categories and product names are PROFECO's own spelling. Store names, addresses and coordinates are PROFECO's; a wrong coordinate or size is fixed through `data/overrides/qqp.json` with a note, never guessed in code. Chains appear by name only, no logos. A store whose published coordinates are provably another store's (same point, another municipio) is not drawn: it keeps its prices and its card, which says why (`unplaceStores` in the override file).
- **The reader's prices are the reader's.** They sit beside PROFECO's, marked "tu precio" in gold, with their own date in the reader's calendar, and the card states the difference against PROFECO's. They never enter any median or ranking and never leave the browser.
- **PACIC**: the official basket has 24 products; PROFECO's catalogue tags more generic products as PACIC (31 in this build). The basket card says both numbers.
- **Credit**: the footer carries the short form (dataset, agency, link, access date in AAAA-MM-DD, the numeric form Libre Uso MX asks for, so it is never localised); Settings carries the full credit (CC BY 4.0, Libre Uso MX, non-endorsement, "A cómo calcula medianas…"), INEGI's product and edition, and © OpenStreetMap contributors. `docs/research/licenses-2026-10.md` §1.3 has the canonical wording.
- **Spanish first.** Mexican usage: "súper", "mandado", "a cómo", "canasta", "anaquel". English is a translation of the same strings, never a different product.
- **Offline is not absence.** When something cannot be fetched because the device is offline, the app keeps the link and says it has not been downloaded on this device yet; only data that is truly gone sends the reader back to the city with "Eso ya no está en los datos de PROFECO".
- **If a number looks wrong**, the fix is a line in the override file and a rerun, recorded under "Curation" in `docs/PIPELINES.md`, so the next person knows why.
